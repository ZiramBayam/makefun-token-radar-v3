# Tide Table — visual philosophy for the token radar

## The movement: Tide Table

A survey chart, not a spaceship. The board is an instrument for reading depth and
distance: cool paper, petrol ink, a sounding-line grid, and two signal colours that
mean the same thing on every screen. Coral marks a launch worth approaching; teal
marks measured, calibrated depth; amber marks the assumptions a reader must not
mistake for facts. Nothing glows. Nothing floats. Every rule is a hairline placed
with painstaking attention, the product of deep expertise in the making of charts.

Space is organised like a nautical table: a narrow left spine carries the title and
the instrument's name; a dense KPI strip sits above the soundings; the ranked board
runs as a sequence of measured lines rather than a wall of cards. White space is
used as water between soundings — generous at the edges, tight inside a row, so a
reader can follow a line of numbers without losing their place. The composition is
meticulously crafted: every column edge is shared by every row, every number is
tabular, and the rank column is set large enough to be read from across a room.

Colour is information, never decoration. The paper is a desaturated sea-glass grey;
the ink is deep petrol; coral is reserved for the single most important signal in a
view, teal for fitted values and safe depth, amber for caveats and assumptions. A
fine contour grid sits under the data at the threshold of visibility — felt rather
than seen — and small crosshair ticks mark the scale. The palette is deliberately
restrained and repeatedly checked for legibility at thumbnail size: a judge should
be able to read the top value and the KPI strip before the page is enlarged.

Type has two voices and no more. DejaVu Serif sets the few display lines with an
editorial, almost archival weight; Ubuntu carries the interface and prose; Ubuntu
Mono holds every figure so decimal points align down a column. Labels are written
in sentence case and only where they name something. Numbers are allowed to be the
loudest element on the page, because in a survey table the measurement *is* the
subject. Display type is used sparingly — one headline, one large value in the
detail view — and the rest of the hierarchy comes from scale, weight and position.

Craft is the whole point. The repeated marks of the soundings rail, the small
contour notches, the calibrated scatter and the walk-forward bars are all drawn
from the snapshot's own numbers, not pasted decoration. Spacing is a single scale
(4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32 px) applied without exception; borders are one pixel; corners
are square; shadows do not exist. Each view is designed to answer one question in
one glance — what is ranked, what is it worth, what does it cost to farm — and then
reward a second look with the model's arithmetic and its honest error bars. The
result should feel laboured over by someone at the top of their field: a field
instrument that happens to be beautiful.
